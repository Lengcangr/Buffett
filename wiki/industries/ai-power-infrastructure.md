# AI Power Infrastructure

## Metadata

- Last reviewed: 2026-06-09
- Primary example: CEG
- Related companies: CEG, VST, NRG, NEE, ETN, VRT, GEV, PWR

## Industry Question

AI data centers increase electricity demand, but investment returns depend on where profit is captured: power generation, grid capacity, regulated transmission, power equipment, cooling, engineering, or cloud services.

## Profit Pool Map

| Layer | Economic role | Main risk | Example companies |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power producers | Sell energy, capacity, clean attributes, PPAs | regulation, outages, fuel, market prices | CEG, VST, NRG |
| Regulated utilities | Earn allowed return on grid and generation assets | rate cases, political pressure, interest rates | NEE, SO |
| Equipment suppliers | Sell electrical gear, cooling, turbines, grid hardware | valuation, order cyclicality, supply chain | ETN, VRT, GEV |
| EPC / services | Build and maintain infrastructure | labor, execution, project risk | PWR |
| Hyperscalers / data centers | Consume power to sell compute | AI ROI, chip depreciation, power availability | MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, META |

## Buffett-Style Industry Tests

- Is demand contracted or only narrated?
- Who has bargaining power when electricity is scarce?
- Are returns protected by scarcity, regulation, switching costs, or long qualification cycles?
- Is growth funded with real owner earnings or rising leverage?
- Is the current price already capitalizing perfect AI demand?

## Common Valuation Traps

- Treating all AI power exposure as equally valuable.
- Valuing peak commodity power earnings as normal.
- Ignoring maintenance and growth capex.
- Using equipment-company multiples for power producers.
- Calling a stock cheap because it fell from a high, not because normalized owner earnings are undervalued.

## Watchlist Trigger

Refresh the sector when there are large data center PPA announcements, PJM/ERCOT capacity rule changes, nuclear restart approvals, transformer/turbine supply updates, or large hyperscaler capex revisions.
