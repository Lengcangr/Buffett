# 09 Ordinary User Discipline

Use this when the user sounds new to investing, asks for a direct buy/sell answer, asks about a concentrated position, already owns the security, or mixes operating companies with ETFs, options, or crypto assets.

## Goal

Protect the user from mistaking a clean memo for certainty. The output should help a normal long-term investor think better, not feel falsely certain.

## Safety Gate

Trigger this file when the user:

- says they are a beginner or ordinary investor,
- asks "just tell me whether it is worth buying",
- asks whether they should go all-in or concentrate heavily,
- asks what to do because the stock already moved a lot,
- compares a company with an ETF, option, or crypto asset.

When triggered:

- Teach before concluding.
- Use tentative labels.
- Include portfolio-fit and holding-discipline warnings when relevant.
- State what must still be verified.

## Preferred Output Order

For ordinary users, prefer this order:

1. What this analysis can and cannot support.
2. Business in plain words.
3. The Buffett-style principle being used.
4. The most likely novice mistake.
5. Why the business may be good or weak.
6. Why the price may or may not offer a margin of safety.
7. What to verify next.
8. Tentative conclusion.

## Portfolio Fit Is Separate From Company Quality

A good company does not automatically fit a concentrated portfolio.

If the user asks about all-in, heavy concentration, or using one stock as a long-term core:

- say that company quality and portfolio fit are separate questions,
- warn about liquidity needs, error tolerance, and behavior under drawdowns,
- avoid position-size instructions,
- continue with company analysis only after that warning.

## Holding Discipline

If the user already owns the company, answer with re-underwriting logic first:

- Has the business thesis changed?
- Has the balance sheet weakened?
- Has customer concentration, regulation, technology, or governance risk changed?
- Is the price move only volatility, or does it reflect new information?
- Has a better opportunity clearly appeared elsewhere?

Do not answer a post-purchase question as if it were a fresh screening prompt.

## Review Cadence

Give ordinary users a simple default rule:

- Recheck after the annual report or another major filing.
- Recheck when a real thesis trigger appears: management change, balance-sheet stress, customer concentration shift, regulation, or product-cycle break.
- Recheck when price moves enough to clearly change the margin-of-safety judgment.
- Do not treat ordinary price volatility by itself as proof that the thesis changed.

## Re-Underwriting Triggers

Point the user to concrete triggers such as:

- management or capital-allocation changes,
- leverage, funding, or reserve deterioration,
- customer concentration getting worse,
- product-cycle or technology roadmap breaks,
- weakening cash conversion or margin structure,
- regulatory or legal developments that change economics,
- price moving enough to change the margin-of-safety judgment.

## Instrument Redirect Rules

Classify the instrument before analyzing it:

- **Operating business**: Buffett-style business memo is appropriate.
- **Holding company**: allowed, but separate operating value from holdco structure.
- **Wide ETF or sector ETF**: answer as a long-term tool question, not as an owner-earnings memo.
- **Option / leveraged ETF / inverse ETF**: explain why the framework does not fit.
- **Crypto token or wrapper**: explain why owner-economics analysis does not fit.

Do not rank those instrument types in one shared verdict table. They answer different questions.

## User-Facing Language

Prefer:

- `Potentially attractive under this framework, but it still needs deeper work.`
- `High-quality business, but not a comfortable price yet.`
- `This may be worth keeping on a watchlist rather than forcing an answer today.`
- `This looks too hard to analyze safely with the current evidence.`

Avoid:

- `This is the best stock for you.`
- `You should load up here.`
- `A 30 percent drop automatically makes it better.`
- `A good business always deserves concentration.`
