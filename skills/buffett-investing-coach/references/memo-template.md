# Buffett-Style Investment Memo Template

Use this template for final company analysis. Keep it evidence-driven and teach the user the logic. This template is for productive operating businesses and analyzable holding companies, not for options, leveraged wrappers, crypto assets, SPACs, or other non-operating instruments.

## 0. Ordinary-User Safety Box

Include this section whenever the user is new, asks for a direct buy/sell answer, asks about concentration, or already owns the security:

- What this analysis can support.
- What it cannot support.
- Biggest unknowns.
- The easiest novice mistake here.
- If relevant, a short portfolio-fit warning.

## 1. Executive View

Hard rule for outsider-facing work:

- Do not open with a conclusion label, price-status label, or executive verdict.
- First explain the business, the novice trap, and what still must be verified.
- Only after that may you give a shortened executive recap.

- Company, ticker, exchange, reporting currency, fiscal year end.
- Security structure if relevant: share class, ADR ratio, VIE, holdco-opco relationship, or other rights issue.
- One-paragraph business description.
- Separate business-quality verdict from current price attractiveness.
- For advanced underwriting only: principle-level conclusion such as worth continued research, watchlist only, too hard, business quality insufficient, valuation lacks safety, or attractive only below a conservative price/evidence range.
- Top three reasons supporting the conclusion.
- Top three reasons the conclusion could be wrong.
- For outsider-facing work, state what must still be verified before the conclusion can support action.

## 2. Source Audit

Create a compact table:

| Source | Publisher | Date | Used for | Link |
|---|---|---:|---|---|

Include every source that materially supports business description, financials, management, valuation, or risk assessment.

## 3. Business Understanding

Explain:

- How the company makes money.
- Segment and geographic mix.
- Customers, suppliers, distribution, and pricing model.
- Cyclicality, regulation, and technology exposure.
- Whether the business is inside, edge, or outside a reasonable circle of competence.
- Whether the security being analyzed cleanly represents the business the user thinks they are buying.

## 4. Moat And Competitive Position

Assess evidence for:

- Pricing power.
- Cost advantage.
- Switching costs.
- Brand/habit.
- Network or ecosystem effects.
- Regulatory scarcity or distribution advantage.

Separate proven moat evidence from management claims.

## 5. Financial Quality And Owner Earnings

Cover trends over the selected review period:

- Revenue growth and per-share growth.
- Margins and returns on capital.
- Cash conversion and working capital.
- Capex intensity and maintenance-capex uncertainty.
- Balance-sheet strength, debt maturity, and hidden liabilities.
- Share count, buybacks, dividends, issuance, and acquisitions.

If owner earnings cannot be estimated reliably, say why.

## 6. Management And Capital Allocation

Evaluate:

- Management tenure and owner orientation.
- Candor and consistency in communications.
- Capital allocation record: reinvestment, M&A, buybacks, dividends, leverage, issuance.
- Compensation incentives and treatment of minority shareholders.
- Governance, related-party, or control-structure concerns.

## 7. Valuation And Margin Of Safety

If current price data is available:

- Quote price date/time and source.
- Estimate conservative earnings or owner-earnings range.
- Use ranges and scenario checks rather than one precise value.
- State what price or evidence would be needed for a visible margin of safety.
- Assign a price-status label: Attractive, Fair/watchlist, Great business with no visible margin, or Too hard to value.
- For outsider-facing outputs, soften the label: `Potentially attractive under this framework`, `Worth watchlist work`, `Great business but price not yet comfortable`, or `Too hard today`.
- If business quality is high but current price is high, name watchlist triggers: lower price, higher normalized owner earnings, better cash conversion, improved balance sheet, clearer regulation, or better capital allocation evidence.
- If business quality is high but current price is high, add a short `Why this stays on the watchlist` note so the user understands why the company is still worth following.

If price data is unavailable or stale, provide business-quality analysis and list the valuation data needed.

## 8. Risks And Anti-Thesis

Include:

- Permanent capital impairment risks.
- Moat erosion risks.
- Accounting or cash-flow quality concerns.
- Regulatory, geopolitical, commodity, technology, or customer-concentration risks.
- A short bear case that a smart skeptical investor would make.

## 9. Missing Information

List information that would materially change the conclusion, such as segment economics, normalized margins, maintenance capex, customer churn, debt maturities, management incentives, or watchlist triggers that would make the price more attractive.

## 10. Re-Underwriting Triggers

List the facts that would require a fresh look:

- Thesis-improving triggers.
- Thesis-breaking triggers.
- Balance-sheet or governance changes.
- Customer concentration or product-cycle changes.
- Events that change whether the company belongs in the watchlist, too-hard pile, or quality bucket.

## 11. Lesson For The User

Explain the Buffett principle being trained:

- What principle was applied?
- What evidence supported it?
- What common novice mistake did the analysis avoid?
- What should the user learn to check next time?

For outsider-facing work, put this section before the final conclusion.

## 12. Final Principle-Level Conclusion

Use one of these forms:

- **Worth continued research**: business quality appears high, and valuation may be close enough to justify deeper work.
- **Watchlist only**: business quality appears high, but current price does not yet provide a visible margin of safety; state what would trigger re-evaluation.
- **Too hard**: economics, accounting, regulation, or technology cannot be understood well enough.
- **Quality insufficient**: evidence does not show durable owner economics.
- **No visible margin of safety**: business may be good, but price or assumptions leave little room for error.
- **Potentially attractive only if**: state the conservative condition, such as a lower price, stronger normalized earnings evidence, or clearer capital allocation.

End with a short note that the memo is educational analysis, not personalized financial advice, and does not answer position sizing or portfolio concentration.
