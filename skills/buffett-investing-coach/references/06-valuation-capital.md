# 06 Valuation And Capital Allocation

Use this when judging current price attractiveness, margin of safety, watchlist status, buybacks, and capital allocation.

## Quality And Price Are Separate

Always state two verdicts:

1. Business quality: is this a business worth understanding and potentially owning?
2. Price status: does the current price offer a visible margin of safety?

A great business at a poor price is not a bad business. It is a watchlist candidate. Research is useful because future price declines, earnings growth, or reduced uncertainty can make it attractive.

## Intrinsic Value Range

Use ranges rather than point estimates.

Build from conservative owner earnings:

- Normalized owner earnings today.
- Sustainable reinvestment rate and return.
- Reasonable growth runway.
- Balance sheet surplus or deficit.
- Terminal durability.
- Required return or earnings yield comparison.

Then test downside:

- No-growth value.
- Margin compression.
- Cycle trough.
- Higher reinvestment need.
- Lower exit multiple.

## Margin Of Safety

Margin of safety is a buffer against being wrong. It should be larger when:

- Business is cyclical.
- Technology/regulation risk is high.
- Management quality is uncertain.
- Accounting is complex.
- Leverage is material.
- Valuation depends on long-duration growth.

It can be smaller only when business quality, durability, and cash flows are unusually certain.

## Price Status Labels

- **Attractive**: conservative assumptions show a visible margin of safety today.
- **Fair/watchlist**: business quality is strong, but margin of safety is thin or uncertain.
- **Great business, no visible margin**: expectations already capitalize too much of the future.
- **Potentially attractive only below**: specify a conservative price/earnings/FCF condition.
- **Too hard to value**: owner earnings cannot be estimated reliably.
- **Not good enough at any normal price**: business quality or stewardship fails first.

## Watchlist Discipline

For a high-quality but expensive company, capture:

- Conservative owner earnings estimate.
- Price or enterprise value that would create a margin of safety.
- Business evidence that could raise intrinsic value.
- Risk evidence that could lower required margin.
- Events that would require re-underwriting.

The watchlist is not procrastination. It is prepared patience.

## Buybacks

Buybacks create value only when shares are repurchased below conservative intrinsic value and do not weaken the balance sheet.

Check:

- Share count actually declines.
- Buybacks are not merely offsetting stock compensation.
- Repurchases are countercyclical or value-sensitive.
- Management explains price discipline.
- Debt is not used recklessly to fund buybacks.

## Acquisitions

Acquisitions require skepticism:

- Price paid versus conservative earning power.
- Organic growth separated from acquired growth.
- Goodwill and impairment risk.
- Integration and culture risk.
- Incentives to grow size.
- Per-share value after financing.

A roll-up can look cheap until accounting and leverage reveal weak economics.

## Ranking Multiple Companies

When the user asks which companies have most potential, rank on two axes:

- Business quality and compounding potential.
- Current price attractiveness and margin of safety.

Do not rank expensive quality companies as bad. Label them as "best businesses to watch" when price is the main obstacle.
