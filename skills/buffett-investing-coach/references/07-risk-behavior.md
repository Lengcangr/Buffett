# 07 Risk And Behavior

Use this when stress-testing the thesis and managing investor behavior.

## Permanent Loss Versus Volatility

A Buffett-style investor worries more about permanent impairment than quoted price volatility. Permanent loss can come from:

- Overpaying for optimistic cash flows.
- Moat erosion.
- Excess leverage.
- Fraud or aggressive accounting.
- Capital allocation mistakes.
- Regulation or technology destroying economics.
- Dilution at bad prices.

Volatility is useful only when it offers good businesses at better prices.

## Behavioral Risks

Watch for analyst errors:

- Falling in love with a great business and ignoring price.
- Overweighting recent growth.
- Using a model to hide uncertainty.
- Confusing complexity with insight.
- Stretching the circle of competence to join a hot theme.
- Treating a famous investor's holding as proof.
- Averaging down without re-underwriting the thesis.

## Too-Hard Pile

Use the too-hard pile when:

- Key economics cannot be understood.
- Normalized earnings cannot be estimated.
- The thesis depends on macro or commodity forecasting.
- Technology transition is too uncertain.
- Regulatory outcomes dominate value.
- Management trust is required but not earned.

Saying "too hard" is a strength, not a failure.

## Pre-Mortem

Before concluding, write the bear case:

- What evidence would prove the moat is weaker?
- What would show earnings are over-normalized?
- What would reveal bad capital allocation?
- What could make the current price dangerous?
- What source would a skeptical expert cite against this thesis?

## Watchlist Behavior

For great but expensive companies:

- Do not force a buy thesis.
- Preserve the research file.
- Define price and evidence triggers.
- Revisit after drawdowns, earnings disappointments, or business improvements.
- Update intrinsic value only when facts change, not because price moved.

## Communication Rules

Be skeptical without being cynical. Acknowledge uncertainty clearly:

- "I can understand this business, but not this price."
- "The business may be excellent, but the margin of safety is not visible."
- "This is outside the circle of competence unless these facts are resolved."
- "The thesis depends on assumptions a Buffett-style investor would not need to make."
