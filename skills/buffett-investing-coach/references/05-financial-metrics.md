# 05 Financial Metrics

Use this when translating financial statements into owner economics.

## Owner Earnings

Owner earnings approximate cash that can be taken out by owners without weakening the business.

Start with:

- Net income.
- Add back depreciation and amortization when economically appropriate.
- Subtract maintenance capital expenditure.
- Adjust for normalized working capital needs.
- Subtract recurring stock compensation if it is a real owner cost.
- Adjust for one-time gains, restructuring, acquisition accounting, and unusual taxes.

Do not mechanically treat free cash flow as owner earnings. Some companies defend their moat through R&D, marketing, content, inventory, or acquisitions rather than reported capex.

## Maintenance Versus Growth Investment

When management does not disclose maintenance capex, triangulate:

- Long-term depreciation versus capex.
- Capacity additions versus replacement needs.
- Unit volume growth versus capital base growth.
- Peer capital intensity.
- Management commentary.
- Asset age and utilization.

If the split is unclear, use a range and say so.

## Return Metrics

Useful measures:

- ROIC: operating profit after tax divided by invested capital.
- Return on tangible capital: useful when goodwill from acquisitions distorts capital base.
- Incremental ROIC: change in operating profit versus reinvestment over time.
- ROE: useful but can be inflated by leverage.
- Gross margin and operating margin: evidence for pricing power or cost advantage.

Prefer multi-year and cycle-adjusted measures.

## Cash Conversion

Good reported earnings should turn into cash over time. Check:

- Operating cash flow versus net income.
- Free cash flow versus net income.
- Working capital swings.
- Receivable quality and inventory risk.
- Capitalized costs.
- Deferred revenue or customer prepayments.

Weak cash conversion is a major warning unless there is a clear, high-return reinvestment explanation.

## Balance Sheet Safety

Review:

- Net debt and gross debt.
- Interest coverage under normalized and stressed earnings.
- Maturity ladder.
- Lease liabilities.
- Pension and post-retirement obligations.
- Guarantees, off-balance-sheet commitments, customer financing.
- Liquidity and access to capital in a downturn.

A strong business can become a bad investment if leverage removes patience.

## Per-Share Analysis

Owner results accrue per share. Track:

- Diluted share count.
- Revenue per share.
- Earnings per share quality.
- Owner earnings per share.
- Buyback price versus value.
- SBC dilution and repurchase offset.

Aggregate growth with rising share count may not create owner value.

## Normalization

Normalize earnings when:

- Industry is cyclical.
- Commodity prices matter.
- Interest rates affect float or financial income.
- Pandemic/supply shocks distorted margins.
- New capacity is ramping.
- Acquisition accounting changed comparability.

Use ranges, not one exact number.
