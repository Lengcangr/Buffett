# 03 Business Moat

Use this when assessing competitive advantage and durability.

## Moat Definition

A moat is a durable structural advantage that protects returns on capital from competition. It is not brand awareness, market share, size, or growth by itself.

A moat should answer:

- Why can this company earn attractive returns?
- Why have competitors failed to erode those returns?
- Why should the advantage persist?
- What evidence would prove the moat is weakening?

## Moat Types And Evidence

| Moat type | Evidence to seek | False positives |
|---|---|---|
| Brand and habit | Repeat purchase, price premium, stable share, low promo dependence | Fame without pricing power |
| Cost advantage | Unit cost gap, scale purchasing, logistics, process know-how | Temporary input-price benefit |
| Switching cost | Mission-critical workflow, high migration risk, embedded data/process | Contract lock-in with low renewal value |
| Network effect | More users increase value and monetization durability | User growth with weak economics |
| Distribution | Hard-to-replicate channels, shelf space, dealer/service network | Paid acquisition or channel stuffing |
| Regulated scarcity | Licenses, rights-of-way, permits, infrastructure constraints | Political favor that can reverse |
| Ecosystem | Complementary products, developer/customer lock-in, high attach | Bundling without customer dependence |

## Numeric Tests

Moats usually leave traces:

- Gross margin stability relative to peers.
- Operating margin resilience through cycles.
- Return on invested capital above cost of capital.
- Low churn or high renewal where disclosed.
- Cash conversion over a full cycle.
- Market share stability without excessive reinvestment.

No single metric proves a moat. Use a mosaic.

## Moat Decay

Watch for:

- Price cuts needed to maintain volume.
- Rising customer acquisition cost.
- Higher capex/R&D/marketing just to stand still.
- New entrants taking the most profitable niche.
- Regulation commoditizing the product.
- Platform shifts changing distribution.
- Key suppliers or customers capturing economics.

## Cyclical Moats

Some businesses look moaty at cycle peaks. Normalize returns across weak years. Ask whether high returns come from structural advantage or shortage conditions.

## Technology Moats

Technology can create moats through switching costs, ecosystem depth, network effects, data advantages, or cost curves. It can also destroy moats quickly. Require extra evidence:

- Customer dependence beyond product novelty.
- Backward compatibility or ecosystem lock-in.
- Cost of migration.
- R&D productivity.
- Ability to adapt without destroying owner economics.

## Moat Verdicts

Use these labels:

- **Proven durable moat**: long evidence across cycles and competitors.
- **Likely moat**: strong evidence, but shorter history or unresolved threat.
- **Emerging moat**: promising but not yet proven in returns/cash flow.
- **Cyclical advantage**: economics depend on supply/demand cycle.
- **No visible moat**: claims lack evidence.
- **Moat too hard**: advantage depends on technology/regulation/forecasting outside the analyst's competence.
