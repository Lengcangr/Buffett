# 02 Investment Philosophy

Use this when judging whether a company resembles the kind of business a Buffett-style investor would want to understand deeply.

## The Ideal Business

A high-quality business tends to have:

- Durable demand that does not require constant reinvention.
- Pricing power or structural cost advantage.
- High returns on incremental capital.
- Cash conversion that is not an accounting illusion.
- Low leverage need.
- Capable, candid, owner-oriented management.
- Reinvestment runway or disciplined return of excess capital.

Quality is not a slogan. It must appear in per-share economics over time.

## Quality Before Cheapness

A bad business at a low multiple can still destroy owner value. First classify business quality, then judge price.

Use four buckets:

- **Compounder candidate**: high-quality business with durable reinvestment runway.
- **Quality cash generator**: strong business but limited reinvestment; value depends on cash return and capital allocation.
- **Cyclical or commodity**: economics depend on cycle position; normalize hard.
- **Too hard or low quality**: no need to force valuation.

## Wonderful Company, Fair Price

A wonderful company can deserve a higher multiple when owner earnings are durable and reinvestment is attractive. But the principle is not permission to ignore price.

Separate:

- Business admiration: the company may be excellent.
- Investment attractiveness: the current price must still leave room for error.
- Watchlist value: research is useful even when the price is too high today.

## Float, Leverage, And Financial Strength

Buffett-style analysis favors resilient balance sheets because they protect optionality. For insurers, float can be valuable if underwriting is disciplined and cost of float is low. For non-financials, leverage is usually dangerous when paired with cyclicality or fragile demand.

Check:

- Debt maturity schedule.
- Interest coverage under a downturn.
- Pension, lease, guarantee, and off-balance-sheet obligations.
- Whether management uses debt to buy time or to increase per-share value.

## Per-Share Owner Outcomes

Aggregate revenue or earnings growth can mislead when share count rises or acquisitions dilute economics. Focus on per-share value creation:

- Revenue per share.
- Owner earnings per share.
- Book value per share where relevant.
- Share count trend.
- Buyback price discipline.
- Return on retained earnings.

## Mistakes As Data

A Buffett-style analyst studies mistakes. Useful categories:

- Buying a mediocre business because it looks cheap.
- Underestimating technology or distribution change.
- Overpaying with stock.
- Trusting reported earnings without cash conversion.
- Letting leverage or float mask poor economics.

When using a case, identify the principle, the evidence, and the limit of the analogy.

## Learning Output

For educational answers, include:

- The principle being applied.
- The company evidence that supports or contradicts it.
- What a novice might overemphasize.
- What an owner-analyst would check next.
