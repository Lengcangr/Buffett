## Shared Deep Report Checks

Every industry report must include:

- Industry profit pool: who captures economics and who bears risk.
- Value-chain position: upstream, midstream, downstream, platform, distributor, balance sheet, or regulated asset.
- Peer comparison: 3-5 peers or substitutes, with limitations.
- Key metrics: 5-year trend where available.
- Valuation traps: what makes cheapness misleading in this industry.
- Anti-thesis: the strongest skeptical case.
- Investor action panel: price/evidence triggers, thesis-breaking signs, and next review event.

## AI Power Infrastructure

Use for CEG, VST, NRG, ETN, VRT, PWR, GE Vernova, regulated utilities, nuclear owners, grid equipment suppliers, and AI data center power-chain companies.

Focus areas:

- Business role: power producer, regulated utility, equipment supplier, EPC contractor, grid bottleneck asset, or data center power partner.
- Demand driver: AI load growth, electrification, reshoring, grid modernization, capacity market, or replacement cycle.
- Contract quality: PPA term, customer credit, price escalators, capacity payments, merchant exposure, and counterparty concentration.
- Asset scarcity: nuclear, gas peakers, transmission access, interconnection queues, turbine supply, transformer supply, or skilled labor.
- Regulation: FERC, state utility commissions, PJM/ERCOT/ISO market rules, permits, nuclear licenses, rate cases.
- Financial checks: EBITDA to FCF conversion, maintenance vs growth capex, leverage, hedging, outage risk, fuel exposure, and acquisition integration.
- Valuation traps: high multiple justified by AI narrative without contracted cash flow; low multiple caused by merchant power volatility or leverage.
- Peer lens: compare at least one direct asset peer, one regulated/utility peer if relevant, and one equipment or infrastructure adjacent company if the thesis is AI power scarcity.
- Action panel triggers: new long-term data center contracts, capacity market changes, nuclear/gas outage data, regulatory approvals, capex revisions, leverage changes, and buyback discipline.

## Semiconductors

Use for NVDA, AMD, AVGO, TSM, ASML, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, QCOM, TXN, ADI, MU, ON, MRVL, ARM, and similar companies.

Focus areas:

- Value-chain position: IP, EDA, fabless design, foundry, equipment, materials, memory, analog, OSAT, distribution, or end-product exposure.
- Cycle normalization: separate secular demand from inventory and capex cycles.
- Moat evidence: process leadership, ecosystem lock-in, switching costs, installed base, software stack, patents, customer qualification, supply chain scale.
- Customer concentration: hyperscalers, Apple, auto, handset, industrial, China, export-controlled customers.
- Financial checks: gross margin durability, R&D intensity, capex intensity, inventory turns, backlog quality, share count, and FCF through cycle.
- Regulation: export controls, China exposure, CHIPS subsidies, geopolitical supply-chain concentration.
- Valuation traps: peak-cycle margins treated as normal, AI demand extrapolated too far, low multiples in structurally weak or commodity names.
- Peer lens: compare within the same value-chain layer first; avoid ranking memory, equipment, foundry, and fabless companies as if they have the same economics.
- Action panel triggers: inventory turns, gross margin trend, capex order strength, major customer capex changes, export-control updates, and normalized earnings revisions.

## Software

Use for MSFT, ADBE, CRM, NOW, INTU, SNOW, DDOG, MDB, cybersecurity, vertical SaaS, and AI software platforms.

Focus areas:

- Business model: subscription, consumption, seat-based, transaction, marketplace, advertising, services, or hybrid.
- Retention economics: gross retention, net revenue retention, churn, expansion, seat growth, usage growth.
- Moat evidence: switching costs, workflow lock-in, data network effects, distribution, ecosystem, developer adoption, brand trust.
- AI impact: whether AI expands value, compresses pricing, increases compute cost, or commoditizes features.
- Financial checks: revenue growth quality, gross margin, sales efficiency, R&D efficiency, SBC dilution, FCF margin, deferred revenue, remaining performance obligations.
- Valuation traps: high growth bought with excessive SBC or sales spend; low multiple caused by churn, weak product differentiation, or platform displacement.
- Peer lens: compare companies with similar go-to-market, retention, margin profile, and customer base.
- Action panel triggers: NRR, billings/RPO, AI monetization, cloud gross margin, SBC dilution, customer concentration, and pricing changes.

## Consumer

Use for branded products, retail, restaurants, apparel, consumer staples, luxury, and durable consumer franchises.

Focus areas:

- Business model: brand owner, retailer, marketplace, distributor, restaurant operator, franchise system, or private-label competitor.
- Demand quality: frequency, habit, category growth, trade-down risk, discretionary vs staple, price elasticity.
- Moat evidence: brand, shelf space, distribution, scale purchasing, loyalty, location, franchise economics, product differentiation.
- Channel economics: wholesale vs DTC, e-commerce, retail partners, franchisees, inventory risk, returns.
- Financial checks: same-store sales, volume vs price mix, gross margin, inventory turns, working capital, advertising intensity, ROIC, buybacks.
- Valuation traps: margin temporarily inflated by price increases, brand decline hidden by promotions, cheap retailer facing structural traffic decline.
- Peer lens: compare category, price tier, channel mix, and international exposure.
- Action panel triggers: volume trend, pricing power, inventory quality, promotional intensity, channel conflict, brand health, and management reinvestment.

## Financials

Use for banks, insurers, payment networks, exchanges, asset managers, ratings agencies, and credit platforms.

Hard rule:

- Do not give a confident Buffett-style conclusion without sector-specific balance-sheet and risk metrics. If key metrics are missing, classify the company as `too hard` or `insufficient evidence`.

Focus areas:

- Business model: spread lender, fee network, insurer, exchange, asset manager, data/rating provider, payment toll road, or specialty finance.
- Balance-sheet quality: capital ratio, leverage, liquidity, funding mix, asset duration, credit quality, reserve adequacy, and maturity mismatch.
- Earnings quality: net interest margin, underwriting profit, float cost, fee revenue quality, transaction volume, AUM sensitivity, loss ratios.
- Moat evidence: network effects, regulatory license, customer trust, data scale, switching costs, brand, distribution.
- Management checks: risk discipline, underwriting culture, reserve conservatism, capital returns, acquisition discipline.
- Valuation traps: low P/B due to credit losses or bad duration risk; high ROE from leverage rather than superior economics.
- Peer lens: compare only similar balance-sheet models or fee models. Do not compare a bank, payment network, and insurer with one generic multiple.
- Action panel triggers: capital ratios, credit losses, reserve development, funding cost, regulatory changes, float cost, and capital return policy.

## Healthcare

Use for pharma, biotech, medtech, diagnostics, life science tools, healthcare services, and device companies.

Focus areas:

- Business model: patented drug, device, consumables, diagnostics, services, tools, payer/provider, or platform pipeline.
- Durability: patent life, product concentration, procedure volume, installed base, reimbursement, replacement cycle, consumable pull-through.
- Moat evidence: clinical evidence, regulatory approvals, physician workflow, switching costs, brand trust, manufacturing quality, distribution.
- Pipeline and innovation: R&D productivity, trial risk, patent cliff, label expansion, acquisition dependence.
- Financial checks: organic growth, gross margin, R&D intensity, FCF conversion, product concentration, litigation, debt, buybacks and M&A returns.
- Regulation: FDA/EMA approvals, reimbursement pressure, pricing pressure, safety warnings, healthcare policy.
- Valuation traps: cheap because of patent cliff, litigation, reimbursement cuts, or failed pipeline; expensive because one product is extrapolated too far.
- Peer lens: compare by product type and risk profile. Avoid treating pharma, medtech, and life-science tools as one peer group.
- Action panel triggers: trial results, approvals, reimbursement changes, product concentration, patent events, safety signals, and M&A discipline.
