# 04 Management And Governance

Use this when evaluating stewardship, incentives, and capital allocation.

## Owner-Oriented Management

A Buffett-style analyst wants managers who think and act like long-term owners. Evidence matters more than charisma.

Check:

- Long tenure with measurable per-share value creation.
- Clear explanation of mistakes and tradeoffs.
- Conservative accounting and non-promotional language.
- Rational reinvestment, acquisitions, buybacks, dividends, and debt use.
- Compensation tied to per-share economics, not empire building.
- Fair treatment of minority shareholders.

## Candor Test

Read multiple years of letters, annual reports, and call transcripts. Look for:

- Does management discuss bad news directly?
- Are metrics consistent over time?
- Are failures explained without excuses?
- Are adjustments reasonable or promotional?
- Does management explain capital allocation in owner terms?

Promotional management can make a good business less investable.

## Capital Allocation Scorecard

Evaluate each dollar retained by the company:

- Reinvestment in the core business: high return and durable?
- Acquisitions: price paid, integration, organic versus acquired growth.
- Buybacks: done below conservative intrinsic value or just to offset dilution?
- Dividends: sensible when reinvestment runway is limited?
- Debt repayment: appropriate for cyclicality and risk?
- Share issuance: value-accretive or dilutive?

Focus on per-share results, not company size.

## Incentives And Governance

Review proxy/circular materials:

- Insider ownership and economic alignment.
- Option/SBC dilution and performance hurdles.
- Related-party transactions.
- Dual-class control or VIE structures.
- Board independence and capital allocation expertise.
- Change-in-control provisions.

Governance weakness demands a larger margin of safety or a pass.

## Red Flags

- Constant metric changes.
- Adjusted earnings that exclude recurring costs.
- Large acquisitions near cycle peaks.
- Buybacks at high valuations while issuing stock compensation.
- Leverage used to defend growth narrative.
- Founder control without owner treatment of minorities.
- Refusal to disclose segment economics needed to understand the business.

## Management Verdicts

Use these labels:

- **Owner-operator quality**: evidence of per-share value creation and candor.
- **Capable operator, uncertain allocator**: business execution good, capital record mixed.
- **Promotional or empire-building risk**: incentives/language/action favor size over owners.
- **Governance discount needed**: structure or related-party risk hurts minority owners.
- **Insufficient evidence**: do not infer quality from reputation alone.
